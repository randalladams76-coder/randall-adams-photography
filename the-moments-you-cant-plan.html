WEDDING PHOTOGRAPHY · BEHIND THE FRAME

The Moments
You Can't Plan.

Why the photographs I love most are often the ones I never expected to take.

This photograph was never planned. There was no pose to arrange, no direction to give and no attempt to recreate a particular moment. It simply happened.

The bride and groom were walking together, and as I watched them, two little girls carrying the bride's veil began making their way into the frame.

I could very easily have missed it.

Had I been standing in one position, simply waiting for the couple to approach, I might never have noticed what was unfolding just behind them. But photography, particularly wedding photography, requires more than pointing a camera at the obvious subject. It requires observation, awareness and an understanding of everything happening around you.

People sometimes ask me how I would describe my style as a photographer. My answer would be journalistic.

The formal family photographs, the carefully arranged portraits and the traditional poses all have their place. They are important, and I believe every couple deserves those photographs with the people closest to them.

But what excites me most are the photographs that happen in between.

The unexpected expressions, the fleeting interactions, the little details and the moments that nobody thought to arrange. These are the photographs that often tell a much bigger story.

In this frame, the bride and groom are naturally the centre of attention, but look a little further. Those two little girls, carrying the bride's veil as they follow behind, add another layer to the photograph. They bring movement, innocence and a beautiful sense of occasion.

Nothing about it needed to be staged.

For me, this is one of the greatest joys of wedding photography. You can plan your equipment, understand the light and anticipate what might happen next, but you can never completely plan the moments that make a wedding day unique.

Sometimes the photograph you didn't set out to take becomes the one you are most grateful you noticed.

Because the story of a wedding isn't only found in the moments everyone is looking at. Sometimes, it's happening just outside the frame.
